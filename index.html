THE BIG QUESTION ↗
What if climate change didn’t just make summers hotter—but made them longer?

Our project investigates how a temperature-based definition of summer and winter may shift across cities under different Shared Socioeconomic Pathways (SSPs). This prototype introduces the story and tests its core interactions while the city-level daily data are being prepared.

01 / WHAT WE'VE DONE SO FAR

The foundation is here.

We have organized the initial website into a location explorer, a scenario comparison, and an explanation of our season-definition method. The global scenario chart uses published CanESM5 CMIP6 temperature summaries and supports interactive scenario highlighting and decade selection. The location section provides a working city selector and shows how the future city-based experience will be structured. We also built an interactive schematic demonstrating how a fixed temperature threshold can change the measured length of summer.

02 / MOST CHALLENGING PART

Turning climate grids into local seasons.

The main challenge is extracting daily temperature series for individual cities from the large, cloud-hosted CMIP6 dataset. We need to establish a consistent 1950–1979 baseline and calculate thresholds using the same units and geographic selection rules across every scenario. A further design challenge is handling brief threshold crossings and winter seasons that span calendar years without producing misleading durations. Finally, we must make the results easy to understand while clearly communicating that SSPs are possible futures rather than certain predictions.
